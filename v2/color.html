Colour

Colour is how people recognise us before they read a word. Purps leads: it is the colour that says Headout. Around it sits a wider palette of expression colours and gradients drawn from real experiences. Used with intent, colours and gradients do more than decorate. They lift a layout, set a mood and elevate everything they touch.

Primary palette

Three colours do most of the work. Purps is our signature, the one colour that is unmistakably Headout. White gives it space and keeps layouts clean and easy to read. Slate black carries text and detail, and grounds everything else. Start here before reaching for any other colour.

Headout Purps in use

Purps is how people know it's us. It leads wherever someone meets Headout for the first time: a billboard, the app icon, a storefront, the first screen. Leading doesn't mean filling every surface, though. A series, a campaign or a busy layout can carry the brand without being purple throughout. Use Purps where recognition matters most, and it keeps its strength.

Secondary palette

If Purps is how people recognise us, these are how we express ourselves. The secondary colours bring warmth, mood and variety, and balance Purps so the brand never feels one-note. They come into play once someone already knows they're with Headout: inside the product, across a campaign, deeper into a journey. Each colour has its own range of tints, tones and shades, so there is always a lighter or deeper option to work with.

Secondary colour in use

Our expression colours at work. Each piece picks one colour and commits to it, across posters, product screens and campaigns, so the result feels vivid and still clearly Headout.

Gradients

Our gradients borrow their colour from real experiences. That is what keeps them feeling natural, never manufactured. They bring warmth and a sense of place to a surface, and give the brand room to feel alive. Let them set the mood, and let the content lead.

One colour, one world

Every colour in our palette can hold a piece of work together. Start with one, and everything around it falls into step. Go on, drag the dot and watch it happen.

Colour use with a purpose

Every colour earns its place. Colour does four jobs across the system — recognition, expression, navigation and utility.
